Step 00 · you, in her Google account

Fix alert delivery before anything else

Blocking

No mail from googlealerts-noreply@google.com has ever arrived in that inbox, though thirty alerts demonstrably exist. Producing a hundred and fifty more queries before that is understood just multiplies a channel that delivers nothing.

This also decides whether the keyword sheet is worth much. Alerts are the only channel that reaches Vogue India, GQ India, AD and Condé Nast Traveller — the trade feeds don't carry them and the search index here barely indexes them.

Work through these in order and stop as soon as one explains it.

  1. In her Gmail, search in:anywhere from:googlealerts-noreply@google.com. The tracker only sees the normal inbox — this catches mail sitting in Spam, in All Mail, or auto-archived.
  2. Gmail → Settings → Filters and blocked addresses. A filter that skips the inbox or applies a label would hide every alert without deleting one.
  3. google.com/alerts, signed in as her. Click the pencil on any one alert and open Show options. Check Deliver to — if it reads RSS feed rather than her email address, that is the whole answer. Alerts set to RSS send no mail at all, and we can't read those feeds either: google.com/alerts/feeds/… is robots-blocked.
  4. Confirm the address it delivers to is the same mailbox the tracker reads.

While you have one alert open, set it the way they should all be:

How oftenAt most once a dayDigest, not a drip

Fix one alert, then wait for it to fire and confirm mail lands. Don't bulk-correct thirty on a theory — if the theory is wrong you've made thirty changes and learned nothing.

Step 03 · you, in her Claude

First run — three clients, not thirty

Scope the first run deliberately. She's on Pro, this reads a lot of mail, and every formatting problem we've hit on this project only became visible in the output. Three clients tells you the shape for a fraction of the budget.

These three are chosen because each stresses something different: Raga is a live campaign with heavy press, St. Regis Goa has a residency running 4–6 September with a visiting chef, and Serious Slice is a small independent whose name is two common words.

Step 04 · you, reading the output

Five things to check before you trust it

This is the step that decides whether the rest is worth doing. Read the campaign rows first — they're the point of the whole exercise.

✓Campaign keywords name the story, not the brand If St. Regis Goa's campaign row is just "St Regis Goa" again, the skill skimmed subject lines instead of opening releases. You want something like "Jacopo Avigo" "Oliveto".
✓Every campaign row has an Expires date Blank means it will still be searched at Christmas. Evergreen rows should be blank; campaign rows must not be.
✓Generic words are qualified Serious Slice is the test. A bare "Serious Slice" is fine quoted; an unquoted Serious Slice is not, and anything like Slice alone is a fail.
✓The ADD NOW block is bare lines One query per line, no numbering, no priority labels, no commentary. It has to survive being copied straight into Google Alerts.
✓No release text leaked into the sheet Names and campaign titles are published anyway and belong there. Body copy, embargo dates and client contact details do not.

If the campaign rows are weak, say so plainly and ask it to reopen the releases rather than accepting them — that's the one thing this skill exists to get right.

Step 06 · you, in the workbook

Paste the sheet in

Select the HTML table in her Claude's reply, copy, click KEYWORDS!A2, paste. It arrives as real cells because it's an HTML table — this is why the skill doesn't emit tab-separated text, which collapses into one column.

Then sort by Client, and freeze row 1. Nothing else is needed: the tracker reads the columns by name.

Step 07 · you and Nikhila, together

Add the alerts by hand, ESSENTIAL first

The skill deliberately doesn't do this. There's no Google Alerts API, so automating it means clicking a hundred and fifty pieces of standing configuration into her Google account with nobody watching for a query that went in wrong. That costs more to unpick than it saves.

Work from the GOOGLE ALERTS TO ADD NOW block. Add the ESSENTIAL queries only, in batches of about twenty, checking after the first three that Google accepted the quotation marks intact.

Quotation marks are load-bearing

Unquoted words in a Google Alert are ANDed together. Several of the roster's existing alerts had their quotation marks flattened at some point and now match almost nothing. If a query goes in as Mezzo Festival Goa instead of "Mezzo Festival" Goa, it is not the same alert.

If Nikhila would rather I drove the browser for this, that's a separate watched session with her present — worth doing once the list is agreed, not in a scheduled task.

Step 08 · the tracker, on its own

Close the loop

Nothing more to configure. The next daily run reads KEYWORDS automatically: unexpired campaign rows get searched first for the clients that have them, evergreen rows override the static CLIENTS query, and expired rows are skipped.

You'll know it worked when the coverage email starts naming campaign terms in its coverage note rather than just client names — and, more to the point, when a piece turns up that nobody would have found by searching the brand.

Cadence. Re-run the keyword skill weekly, or whenever a batch of releases goes out. Each run also produces a RETIRE THESE list of expired campaign queries — deleting those from Google Alerts is what stops the inbox filling with festivals that ended in September. An alert list nobody prunes is how the last setup died.
